Slow as molasses — until it isn't

On 15 January 1919 a storage tank in Boston's North End let go and 2.3 million gallons of molasses came out at an estimated 35 mph. The interesting part is why a famously slow fluid behaved like a tsunami — and then why it turned back into tar.

2.3 millionUS gallons of molasses in the tank (≈ 8,700 m³)

~8 mpeak height of the wave; it ran about two blocks

21people killed; about 150 injured
1925the year a court auditor ruled the owners negligent

The physics, in one slider

Molasses is a shear-thinning fluid whose viscosity changes enormously with temperature — researchers measured roughly a hundredfold change across the range relevant to that January. Drag the temperature and watch two drops race down the same slope.
